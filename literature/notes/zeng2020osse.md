## Problem
A NOAA Science Advisory Board task force review of how OSSEs are used in the US: their
value, their limitations, and what the community should do next.

## Method
White-paper synthesis / community review, not an experiment.

## Data
Existing OSSE literature and US operational practice.

## Outputs
Five recommendations, of which three matter to us:
1. national coordination of OSSE efforts;
2. consideration of the potential impact **on OSSEs** of deficiencies in the current data
   assimilation and prediction system;
3. **extension of OSSEs to societal impacts**.

It also notes OSSEs can be complemented by forecast-sensitivity-to-observations (FSOI)
calculations, which evaluate many observation types simultaneously.

## Key equations
None.

## Assumptions
That OSSE conclusions are conditioned on the quality of the underlying prediction and
assimilation system.

## Validation
Not applicable.

## WildfireGuardian overlap
Directly on WG-C-010. This paper is the closest thing to an authoritative statement of
where decision/impact-level OSSEs stood as of 2020, and it lists extension to societal
impacts as an *outstanding recommendation* rather than as accomplished work.

## WildfireGuardian difference
**Requirement imposed on us:** two of them.
1. Frame WG-C-010 as an instance of this community's own open recommendation —
   "extension of OSSEs to societal impacts" — with wildfire evacuation decisions as the
   impact. This is a much stronger and more honest framing than "first OSSE for wildfire
   decisions", and it comes with a citation.
2. Adopt recommendation (2) as a stated limitation: our OSSE's conclusions are conditioned
   on the deficiencies of the fire model and the decision model inside it. Say so in the
   paper, before a reviewer says it for us.
