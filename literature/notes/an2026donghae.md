## Problem
Do disaster-vulnerable residents actually move when a Korean wildfire evacuation text
message tells them to, and how fast?

## Method
Observational. Two neighbourhoods (Mangsang-dong, Mukho-dong, Donghae) that received the
*same* emergency text guidance during the 2022 Donghae wildfire. Baseline = average floating
population by weekday and hour over the three weeks before the fire. Two derived measures:
- **response time** = first moment after the initial emergency message at which the
  percentage change in floating population exceeds 0% relative to baseline;
- **movement intensity** = maximum percentage increase in floating population over the
  following four hours.

## Data
SK Telecom mobile floating-population data, 2022 Donghae wildfire, disaggregated by group
(domestic residents, long-term foreign residents, older adults).

## Outputs
- Long-term foreign residents: **shorter** response time and **greater** movement intensity
  than domestic residents.
- Older adults: response time relatively short in some areas but **movement intensity
  consistently the lowest of all groups**.
- Interpretation offered by the authors: older adults' evacuation vulnerability is
  associated less with failing to receive/recognise the information than with difficulty
  carrying out the movement itself.

## Key equations
Two operational definitions (response time, movement intensity) as above; no model.

## Assumptions
Floating-population change is a valid proxy for evacuation movement; the pre-fire
three-week average is a valid counterfactual; group labels from carrier data are accurate.

## Validation
The measurement *is* the observation — a real fire, real mobility data. No model to
validate. External validity beyond two dong is untested.

## Limitations
Two neighbourhoods, one fire; aggregate cell-level data cannot see individuals, vehicles,
or whether anyone assisted; no fire-arrival time is paired with the movement, so no lead
time or margin can be recovered from it as published.

## WildfireGuardian overlap
This is the best **empirical Korean evidence for the premise underlying WG-C-005**: in a
real Korean wildfire, under identical warning content, older residents moved least. It is
exactly the "cannot self-evacuate is a mobility and transport-access condition, not a
diagnosis" framing PROJECT_CONTEXT insists on — and it is measured, not asserted.

## WildfireGuardian difference
An & Choi measure *what happened*; WildfireGuardian computes *what must happen by when*.
No fire model, no deadline, no responder, no counterfactual policy.
