## Problem
Fire-front observations arrive at different times at different places along the perimeter. Stale observations
degrade data assimilation. How should an assimilation scheme handle asynchronous, partly historical observations?

## Method
An Ensemble Transform Kalman Filter variant ("ETKF-distributed") that assimilates a fire-front observation
immediately at whatever location it arrives, using new prediction-to-observation matching schemes, rather than
waiting to batch observations globally. Compared against plain ETKF and an earlier "ETKF-centralised".

## Data
A simulated wildland fire built on real terrain, vegetation and historical local weather.
**Observations were generated by Observing System Simulation Experiments.**

## Outputs
Prediction accuracy retained under asynchronous observation; plus an operational recommendation about where to
put observing effort.

## Key equations
ETKF update equations; not retrieved.

## Assumptions
- The fire spread model used to generate synthetic truth and the model used for prediction share structure
  (classic identical-twin OSSE exposure — see kaipio2007inversecrime and yu2019twin already filed in this repo).
- Observation error is prescribed.

## Validation
OSSE only. No real wildfire assimilation.

## Limitations
Synthetic truth; single fire scenario; no decision layer of any kind.

## WildfireGuardian overlap
Two overlaps, both uncomfortable and both worth stating before a judge does:
1. **OSSE (WG-C-010).** This paper uses OSSE by name to evaluate wildfire fire-front estimation. OSSE in wildfire
   is not new. What is unclaimed is OSSE evaluating an *evacuation decision*.
2. **Observation allocation (WG-C-008).** The paper concludes that observing resources should be concentrated in
   regions of high spread rate. That is an observation-targeting rule — but it is derived from where the
   *prediction* benefits most, not from where a *protective-action decision* would change.

## WildfireGuardian difference
Operational, and it is the whole difference for WG-C-008: Zha et al. target observations at the fastest-spreading
fire front. WildfireGuardian would target observations at the front segment whose uncertainty most affects whether
a responder's round trip still closes — which may be a slow-spreading segment sitting across the only ingress road.
The two rules select different pixels, and we should be able to show a case where they disagree.

## Novelty threat
**HIGH for WG-C-010** (OSSE is established practice in wildfire, so "we use an OSSE" is not a contribution) and
**MODERATE for WG-C-008** (observation allocation in wildfire exists; decision-consequence-driven allocation does
not appear in this work).
