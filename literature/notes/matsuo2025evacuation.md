# matsuo2025evacuation

## Citation
Matsuo, Y., Kietzman, K., Hays, R. D., & Song, Y. (2025). "Evacuation and Transportation Barriers
Among Vulnerable Populations in Natural Hazard-Related Disasters: A Scoping Review."
*International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health*, 22(11), 1680.
DOI: 10.3390/ijerph22111680.

## Publication status
`PEER_REVIEWED`, journal article, open access (GOLD). Abstract retrieved in full via Semantic
Scholar; article body read via PMC (PMC12652914) through WebFetch.

## Problem
What is actually known about transportation and evacuation barriers for older adults, people with
disabilities, and people with chronic health conditions in wildfires, hurricanes, earthquakes and
floods — and what are the research gaps.

## Method
PRISMA-ScR scoping review across five databases (PubMed, APA PsycINFO, CINAHL Complete, EMBASE,
Web of Science). Twenty primary studies included. The **STEPS framework** (Spatial, Temporal,
Economic, Physiological, Social) structures the analysis.

## Data
Twenty primary studies, predominantly qualitative (interviews, focus groups) and cross-sectional
surveys of disaster survivors, collected **post-event**.

## Outputs
Four areas of concern: (1) immediate transportation barriers during evacuation; (2) prolonged
transportation disruptions post-disaster; (3) anticipated logistical challenges in future
evacuation planning; (4) inconsistent and inaccessible communication of transportation information
during emergencies.

## Key equations
None — this is a narrative synthesis, not a model.

## Assumptions
Target population defined *categorically*, not operationally: "older adults (55 years old and
above), adults of any age with disabilities or chronic illnesses, or official/unofficial support
personnel for these populations."

## Validation
Not applicable. PRISMA-ScR protocol adherence is the quality control.

## Limitations
**Directly relevant to WildfireGuardian:** the review provides **no operational definition of
"cannot self-evacuate."** It reports barrier *categories*, not a testable predicate. The evidence
base is retrospective and self-reported, and — per the reading of the full text — **no population
registry or baseline census instrument that systematically identifies evacuation-disadvantaged
persons in advance is reported.**

This is a finding, not a complaint: it means WildfireGuardian's population predicate cannot be
lifted from the review and must be constructed and justified.

## Outputs relevant to the "cannot self-evacuate" definition
Barrier criteria actually reported (these are the closest thing the literature offers to an
operational definition, and they are transport/mobility criteria, not diagnoses):
- **No private vehicle / does not drive** — reported figure: 43% of participants in one included
  study did not drive, and 50% indicated a lack of access to public transportation.
- **Mobility limitation** — requires assistive devices (canes, wheelchairs).
- **Sensory impairment** — hearing or vision loss (affects receiving the evacuation message).
- **Equipment dependency** — dependence on life-sustaining equipment such as ventilators.
- **Caregiver reliance** — depends on family, caregivers or medical staff for transportation
  and support.

## WildfireGuardian overlap
None methodologically. This is the **evidence base** WildfireGuardian's population assumption must
rest on, and the paper WildfireGuardian should cite when a judge asks "how do you know anyone
actually cannot self-evacuate?"

## WildfireGuardian difference
WildfireGuardian must convert these categorical barriers into a **transport-access predicate**
usable in a model — e.g. "household has zero available drivers or zero available vehicles at the
decision time." Note that three of the five criteria above (sensory impairment, equipment
dependency, caregiver reliance) are physiological or medical. PROJECT_CONTEXT forbids medicalising
residents, so WildfireGuardian must use only the transport-access subset (no vehicle / no driver /
mobility-device use requiring an accessible vehicle) — and must **say explicitly** that it is
adopting a narrower predicate than the literature uses, and why.
